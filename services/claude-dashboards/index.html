Proof: twenty golden questions

A dashboard is only useful once you know it is right. So before handover, your owner and your accountant agree about twenty questions and their correct answers, worked out from the books. Then we ask Claude, in plain words, with no hints.

Here is the worked example on Wide World Importers, Microsoft's public sample company, loaded into BigQuery as raw sales tables: invoices, invoice lines, orders, payments and customers, exactly as an operational system keeps them.

Claude Dashboards answered all twenty correctly. On the three questions with a second fair reading, such as whether follow-up backorders count as new orders, it gave both answers and said which one it used.

Why golden questions, not a demo

A demo shows that a chart appears. A golden-question scorecard shows that the number on it is the one your accountant would sign. It also catches the moment a data load breaks, a definition drifts, or a new system arrives, because the scorecard stops matching.

After the quick-start

Most teams then want every dashboard to use the same definitions, and a monthly check. That is Trusted numbers for Claude and Copilot.
